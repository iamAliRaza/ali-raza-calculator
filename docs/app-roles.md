# App Roles

### Student
A **Student** is a university student taking a course whose final grade is made up of several weighted assessments, such as quizzes, assignments, a midterm and a final exam. Partway through the term, some assessments have been marked and others are still to come. They are unsure where they stand and how hard they need to work on what's left.

They can enter each assessment's weight, maximum points and, once marked, the points they earned, leaving upcoming assessments blank. They can see their current grade on completed work as a percentage and a letter. They can set a target grade and see the percentage they need on each remaining assessment to reach it.

They must never be told a target is achievable when it would need more than 100% on the remaining work. They must never be shown a required score calculated from weights that do not total 100%, or a grade calculated from invalid marks, such as more points earned than the maximum.

### Instructor
An **Instructor** is a lecturer or teaching assistant who has marked a course's assessments and must calculate final grades. This often means checking one student at a time: a borderline result, a grade a student has questioned, or the effect of a regrade.

They can enter the course's assessment breakdown and one student's marks. They can see the final weighted grade to two decimal places with its letter grade, and change any mark to see immediately how the result changes.

They must never be shown a result labelled as a final grade while any assessment is unmarked or the weights do not total 100%. They must never see a letter grade that does not match the percentage displayed next to it.

---

### How their needs differ

| | Student | Instructor |
|---|---|---|
| **Main question** | "What do I need on the rest?" | "What is this student's final grade?" |
| **Typical data** | Partial, with future assessments blank | Complete, with every assessment marked |
| **Key output** | Required score on remaining work | Exact final grade and letter |
| **Cost of a wrong answer** | Poor study planning | A wrong grade on an official record |
| **Key protection** | No false hope from impossible targets | No partial grade presented as final |
